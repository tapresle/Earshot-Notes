# Earshot Notes 0.2.3

Released October 17, 2026. Words now appear in the transcript while people speak, summaries cover the whole meeting, and you can replace the instructions the local AI model follows when it writes a summary.

## Requirements

Unchanged from 0.1.0: a Mac with Apple Silicon and macOS 26 or later.

## Upgrading

Open the disk image and drag **Earshot Notes** to **Applications**, replacing the old copy. Your meetings, notes, summaries, chat, models, settings, and permissions carry over. This release doesn't change the meetings database.

## New

- **See words while people speak:** while you record, the transcript shows what each side is saying as muted, italic text, updated about once a second. When the speaker pauses, the final transcription replaces it in the same place. Only the final text is saved, exported, summarized, or used in chat. ([#169](https://github.com/tapresle/Earshot-Notes/issues/169))
- **Your own summary instructions:** **Settings** > **Summaries** shows the instructions summaries follow. Select **Customize** to edit a copy and **Save** to use it for the next summaries, up to 6,000 characters. **Reset to default** goes back to the built-in instructions, including any changes to them in later releases. The rules for reading the meeting, such as who **Me** is and never inventing facts, always apply after your instructions. ([#161](https://github.com/tapresle/Earshot-Notes/issues/161))

## Improved

- **Summaries reflect the whole meeting:** the model reads the whole transcript before it writes, keeps everything said about a topic under one heading even when the meeting comes back to it, says when an idea was suggested but not agreed, and lists notes the meeting didn't discuss as you wrote them. Personal news people shared, such as a trip or a new role, goes under **Personal notes**. ([#162](https://github.com/tapresle/Earshot-Notes/issues/162))
- **Next steps are always there:** when the summary leaves out its **Next steps** section, the model makes a short second pass that lists the meeting's tasks and who took them on. This applies to the default instructions, and to your own when they ask for **Next steps**. ([#162](https://github.com/tapresle/Earshot-Notes/issues/162))
- **Better titles:** untitled meetings are named after their stated goal or their biggest topic, not whatever was said first, in up to 15 words. ([#162](https://github.com/tapresle/Earshot-Notes/issues/162))
- **Cleaner transcripts:** filler words such as "um" and "uh" are removed, and more phrases that Whisper invents from silence or noise, such as "Thanks for listening." and "Please subscribe.", are dropped. ([#163](https://github.com/tapresle/Earshot-Notes/issues/163))

## Fixed

- **Check for updates looks like a button:** in **Settings** > **Updates**, the button has a border instead of reading as plain gray text. ([#171](https://github.com/tapresle/Earshot-Notes/issues/171))

## Known limitations

- **English only:** transcription is English only. Meetings held in other languages get an English transcript.
- **Call audio with Whistle:** the other participants' audio arrives at phone quality, so with Whistle their side of the transcript has more mistakes than yours, especially with names and technical terms. Whisper large-v3-turbo does much better.
- **Whisper and your vocabulary:** Whisper large-v3-turbo doesn't listen for your **Vocabulary** terms. **Heard as** corrections and the spellings in summaries and answers still apply.
- **Some echo remains without headphones:** echo is recognized by comparing words, so echo that transcription heard too differently from the original can still appear as **Me**. Headphones avoid it entirely. ([#88](https://github.com/tapresle/Earshot-Notes/issues/88))
- **Who said what:** everyone else on a call is one speaker, **Meeting Participants**, so summaries and answers can credit a participant's task to the wrong person. ([#27](https://github.com/tapresle/Earshot-Notes/issues/27))
- **Chat in long meetings:** when a meeting is too long to send to the model whole, chat sends the lines that share words with your question. A line that says the same thing in other words, or that transcription got wrong, can be missed.
